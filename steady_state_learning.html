01 / 05 · THE CONCEPT

What is tracking error?

The controller compares the desired altitude with the actual altitude. Their difference is the tracking error.

\[\boxed{e(t)=r(t)-y(t)}\]

Tracking error changes throughout the response. But what error remains after the system has settled?

02 / 05 · STEADY-STATE ERROR

What error remains after the drone settles?

Steady-state error is the tracking error that remains after the system has settled.

\[\boxed{e_{ss}=\lim_{t\to\infty}e(t)}\]

Example: if the target is 10 m and the drone settles at 9.9 m, \(e_{ss}=10-9.9=0.1\text{ m}\).

How do we calculate it?

The same tracking error can be written in the Laplace domain. Here, R(s) is the desired altitude and Y(s) is the actual altitude:

\[\boxed{E(s)=R(s)-Y(s)}\]

E(s) is not the steady-state error. It is the tracking error in the Laplace domain.

For unity feedback, \(Y(s)=\frac{L(s)}{1+L(s)}R(s)\). Therefore:

\[ E(s)=R(s)-\frac{L(s)}{1+L(s)}R(s) =\boxed{\frac{R(s)}{1+L(s)}} \]

Now use the Final Value Theorem to find the error after settling:

\[\boxed{e_{ss}=\lim_{s\to0}sE(s)}\]

For the 10 m step, \(R(s)=10/s\):

\[e_{ss}=\lim_{s\to0}\frac{10}{1+L(s)}\]

Important: the Final Value Theorem is used only when the relevant closed-loop error dynamics satisfy its stability conditions.

03 / 05 · THE RULE

Count the integrators in the open loop

The system type is the number of uncancelled poles at the origin in the open-loop transfer function \(L(s)\). It helps predict tracking error for step, ramp and parabolic references.

Type 0
\[L(s)=\frac{K}{s+2}\]

No pole at the origin. A step can leave a nonzero error.

Type 1
\[L(s)=\frac{K}{s(s+2)}\]

One pole at the origin. Stable closed loop → zero step error.

Type 2
\[L(s)=\frac{K}{s^2(s+2)}\]

Two poles at the origin. Stable closed loop → zero step and ramp error.

For the drone comparison, the step-reference error and the constant-disturbance response are two different tests. Do not use the wind-on result as the wind-free step-error prediction.

Why is \(E(s)=R(s)-Y(s)\)?

The error is simply the gap between what we want and what we actually get.

1. Identify the two signals

\(R(s)\) = desired drone altitude. \(Y(s)\) = actual drone altitude.

\[\boxed{E(s)=R(s)-Y(s)}\]
2. Substitute the unity-feedback output
\[Y(s)=\frac{L(s)}{1+L(s)}R(s)\]
\[E(s)=R(s)-\frac{L(s)}{1+L(s)}R(s)\]
3. Simplify

Factor out \(R(s)\), then combine the terms inside the bracket:

\[ \begin{aligned} E(s) &=R(s)\left(1-\frac{L(s)}{1+L(s)}\right)\\ &=R(s)\left(\frac{1+L(s)-L(s)}{1+L(s)}\right)\\ &=\boxed{\frac{R(s)}{1+L(s)}} \end{aligned} \]

The \(+L(s)\) and \(-L(s)\) cancel. Here \(L(s)=C(s)G_p(s)\), which represents the controller and plant together.
