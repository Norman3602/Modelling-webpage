Bouncy ride: spring and damper

A car seat, a camera gimbal and a pogo-stick all behave like a mass on a spring and damper. The road moves, and the body follows with H(s) = (2ζωn·s + ωn²) / (s² + 2ζωn·s + ωn²). Bode plot notes

Tap the cards and try the tools.

1 · What is a Bode plot?

Two graphs of a transfer function L(jω) against frequency on a log axis: the magnitude in dB and the phase in degrees. Log axes turn multiplication into addition, so you can draw each factor separately and just add them up.

2 · Decibels (dB)

dB = 20·log₁₀|G|. Every ×10 in gain is +20 dB, every ×2 is about +6 dB, and gain 1 is 0 dB.

Gain |G| = 20.0 dB or dB = gain 10

5 · Reading stability

Gain crossover ωgc: where magnitude crosses 0 dB. Phase margin PM = 180° + phase at ωgc. Phase crossover ωpc: where phase = −180°. Gain margin GM = −(magnitude in dB at ωpc).

Closed loop is stable when PM > 0 and GM > 0 dB. Roughly 45–60° of PM gives a well-damped response.

Phase at ωgc ° → PM = 50° stable
6 · Drawing recipe
  1. Write L(s) in standard form and list corner frequencies.
  2. Start with the low-frequency slope (−20 dB/dec per integrator) and DC gain.
  3. At each pole, slope drops by 20 dB/dec. At each zero, it rises by 20.
  4. Phase moves 45° at the corner, spreading over about one decade each side.
  5. Sum the factors, then read ωgc, PM and GM.
